# Retry Attempts and Final Status

A gRPC client can retry an RPC when a prior attempt encounters a transient error. As a result, a single operation from the caller's perspective can generate multiple attempts at the transport/server layer while producing only one final outcome at the client API. If you only count the final status, you miss retries amplifying load; if you only count per-attempt errors, you may misjudge a call that ultimately succeeded.

**Retry attempts and final status** means separately observing attempt counts, per-attempt outcomes, and the final status of the logical RPC. This lesson belongs to the **Observability** chapter of the **SPRING_GRPC** module: developing gRPC services in the Spring ecosystem using Protocol Buffers contracts, generating Java code, and implementing client/server for unary or streaming RPCs; learners work with metadata, deadlines, statuses, interceptors, TLS, integration testing, and monitoring performance and reliability. The focus is on distinguishing logical calls from attempts, understanding retry commitment and overall deadlines, and designing metrics/traces that do not mix intermediate outcomes with final results.

## 1. Logical Calls and Attempts Are Two Different Units

- **Logical call** is one application invocation of a gRPC method, such as `Inventory.GetStock`.
- **Attempt** is an individual client try to execute the logical call over transport/to a server.
- **Retry** is an additional attempt after a prior attempt, when the error and policy permit retrying.
- **Final status** is the single status the caller receives when the logical call completes.
- **Attempt status** is the outcome of an individual try; a failed attempt may be followed by another attempt.

For example, a client invokes `GetStock` once:

1. Attempt 1 goes to instance A and receives `UNAVAILABLE`.
2. The client waits for backoff, then tries attempt 2 to instance B.
3. Attempt 2 returns a successful response.
4. The application receives final status `OK`.

In this case, there is one logical call, two attempts, one retry, and final status `OK`. Server A may record one call ending with `UNAVAILABLE`; server B records one call ending with `OK`. The client-level logical-call metric records only one final `OK` outcome.

Do not add attempt counts and logical call counts together as "total requests". They measure two different layers. Attempt count reflects the actual load generated by the client; logical call count reflects invocation intent at the application layer.

## 2. Retries Do Not Always Happen

gRPC retries require a matching policy and that the attempt has not yet been committed. Key factors:

- The method has a retry policy in the service config.
- The attempt's status is in `retryableStatusCodes`.
- The attempt count has not exceeded `maxAttempts`.
- The overall deadline still has enough remaining time for backoff and the next attempt.
- The call has not been committed under gRPC's retry rules.
- Retry throttling or server pushback does not require stopping retries.

Without a retry policy, gRPC may still perform limited transparent retries when transport knows the request has not yet been processed by the server. Transparent retries differ from policy-driven retries and should be distinguished when reading metrics.

A call can terminate immediately on the first attempt despite a retryable status if no policy applies, the deadline is nearly exhausted, retries are throttled, or the call is already committed. Conversely, a logical call that ultimately returns `OK` may still have generated multiple failed attempts beforehand.

## 3. Retry Commitment and Replay Limits

Retrying is only safe while gRPC can still replay the call according to semantics and buffered data. A call can become committed, after which gRPC stops retrying that call under the policy. Specific commitment conditions depend on the protocol/runtime and can include the client having received response headers or exceeding the retry buffer limit.

Points to keep in mind:

- Before receiving response headers, gRPC may still be able to retry if the attempt ends with a retryable status and the policy permits.
- Once response headers have been delivered to the application, the call is generally committed; do not assume the client will retry that attempt.
- The retry buffer limits how much request data is retained for replay. Exceeding this limit can commit the call.
- For streaming RPCs, messages may have already been sent/received or partially processed; you cannot by default reopen the stream and replay everything without producing duplicate messages.
- A failed attempt does not prove the server has not performed side effects. A response may be lost after the server already committed business state.

Therefore, retries are best suited for idempotent operations or operations with an idempotency key/deduplication. For write RPCs, business design must tolerate the same logical operation arriving at the server multiple times.

## 4. Configuring Retries for a Unary Method

Example service config for the read method `inventory.Inventory/GetStock`:

~~~java
Map<String, ?> retryPolicy = Map.of(
        "maxAttempts", 3.0,
        "initialBackoff", "0.1s",
        "maxBackoff", "1s",
        "backoffMultiplier", 2.0,
        "retryableStatusCodes", List.of("UNAVAILABLE")
);

Map<String, ?> methodConfig = Map.of(
        "name", List.of(Map.of(
                "service", "inventory.Inventory",
                "method", "GetStock")),
        "retryPolicy", retryPolicy
);

Map<String, ?> serviceConfig = Map.of(
        "methodConfig", List.of(methodConfig)
);

ManagedChannel channel = ManagedChannelBuilder
        .forTarget("dns:///inventory.internal:8443")
        .defaultServiceConfig(serviceConfig)
        .build();
~~~

`maxAttempts` includes the initial attempt. With a value of 3, the client has at most three tries for one logical call, meaning at most two retries. Backoff grows according to `initialBackoff` and `backoffMultiplier`, capped at `maxBackoff`; the runtime may also apply jitter/throttling depending on the retry implementation.

The example retries only `UNAVAILABLE`. Do not mechanically include `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, `PERMISSION_DENIED`, or `UNAUTHENTICATED` in retryable statuses because resending the same request/credentials usually does not fix the root cause. `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` should only be retried when semantics and server signals indicate a transient error, alongside backoff and load limits.

In a Spring application, channels are typically managed by a starter or client configuration. Place the service config where the channel is built; do not create a new channel per RPC. Verify the `grpc-java` version and whether the resolver passes service config to the channel.

## 5. Deadlines Apply to the Entire Logical Call

A logical call's deadline covers all attempts and the backoff intervals between them. Retries do not grant a fresh full timeout per attempt:

~~~text
Logical call deadline: 1.0 s

Attempt 1: 0.0 s ---------------- 0.2 s  UNAVAILABLE
Backoff:                       0.2 s -- 0.3 s
Attempt 2:                              0.3 s -------- 0.7 s  UNAVAILABLE
Backoff:                                                    0.7 s -- 0.8 s
Attempt 3:                                                           0.8 s -- 1.0 s
                                                                    deadline
~~~

In the example, attempt 3 only has the remaining time budget until the overall deadline. If the deadline expires during backoff or while an attempt is in flight, the logical call ends and the client typically receives `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`. An earlier attempt may have received `UNAVAILABLE`, yet the logical call's final status is still `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`.

This is why attempt status and final status must be measured separately. If a dashboard only shows final status, it may hide that the service retried multiple times before the deadline expired. If it only shows attempt errors, it may miss that callers ultimately received `OK`.

## 6. Three Status Layers to Observe

A system can have three status perspectives:

| Layer | Unit | Represented Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client logical call | One application invocation of a method | Final status the caller receives in the terminal callback. |
| Client attempt | One try at the transport/server | Outcome of the attempt; may be a retryable error followed by a retry. |
| Server call | One RPC attempt actually received by the server | Status the server used to close the attempt; misses attempts that never reached the server. |

For example, attempt 1 reaches server A and ends with `UNAVAILABLE`, while attempt 2 reaches server B and ends with `OK`:

- Client logical call: one call, final status `OK`.
- Client attempts: two attempts, one `UNAVAILABLE` failure and one success.
- Server calls: two received RPCs, one `UNAVAILABLE` and one `OK`.

If attempt 1 fails before reaching the server, the client may record an attempt failure while the server has no corresponding call. If a response is lost after the server closed with `OK`, the server may record `OK` while the client ultimately observes `UNAVAILABLE` or deadline expiry. Do not expect counters across all three layers to match.

## 7. Metric Model: Logical Calls, Attempts, and Retries

Define metrics separately:

~~~text
grpc.client.calls.started
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method

grpc.client.calls.completed
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method, final_status

grpc.client.attempts.started
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method

grpc.client.attempts.completed
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method, attempt_status

grpc.client.call.retries
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method

grpc.server.calls.completed
  type: Counter
  tags: service, method, server_status
~~~

The names above are illustrative custom metrics; OpenTelemetry instrumentation or the gRPC runtime may use different names and attributes. Keep the semantics clear in metric descriptions:

- `calls.started` increments once when the application creates a logical call.
- `calls.completed` increments once when the logical call receives its final status.
- `attempts.started` increments each time an attempt starts.
- `attempts.completed` increments when an attempt produces an outcome observed by instrumentation.
- `call.retries` increments for each additional attempt after the first attempt.
- `server.calls.completed` increments for each RPC attempt the server actually receives and finishes.

Do not assume a standard `ClientInterceptor` sees every retry attempt. An interceptor at the application boundary typically observes the logical call and final `onClose`; retry machinery may live inside the channel/transport. To get attempt-level metrics, use gRPC/OpenTelemetry instrumentation with retry-attempt support or telemetry from a proxy/control plane; verify the semantics of your deployed version.

## 8. ClientInterceptor Is Suited for Counting Final Status

Example client interceptor counting completed logical calls by final status:

~~~java
public final class LogicalCallMetricsInterceptor
        implements ClientInterceptor {

    private final MeterRegistry registry;

    public LogicalCallMetricsInterceptor(MeterRegistry registry) {
        this.registry = registry;
    }

    @Override
    public <ReqT, RespT> ClientCall<ReqT, RespT> interceptCall(
            MethodDescriptor<ReqT, RespT> method,
            CallOptions callOptions,
            Channel next) {

        String fullMethod = method.getFullMethodName();
        int separator = fullMethod.lastIndexOf('/');
        String service = separator < 0
                ? "unknown"
                : fullMethod.substring(0, separator);
        String methodName = separator < 0
                ? fullMethod
                : fullMethod.substring(separator + 1);

        registry.counter(
                "grpc.client.calls.started",
                "service", service,
                "method", methodName
        ).increment();

        return new ForwardingClientCall
                .SimpleForwardingClientCall<>(
                        next.newCall(method, callOptions)) {
            @Override
            public void start(
                    Listener<RespT> responseListener,
                    Metadata headers) {
                Listener<RespT> monitored =
                        new ForwardingClientCallListener
                                .SimpleForwardingClientCallListener<>(
                                        responseListener) {
                            @Override
                            public void onClose(
                                    Status status,
                                    Metadata trailers) {
                                registry.counter(
                                        "grpc.client.calls.completed",
                                        "service", service,
                                        "method", methodName,
                                        "final_status",
                                        status.getCode().name()
                                ).increment();
                                super.onClose(status, trailers);
                            }
                        };
                super.start(monitored, headers);
            }
        };
    }
}
~~~

This metric records the final status observed by the client application. If a call retries once and then succeeds, the interceptor typically records one completion with `final_status=OK`; it does not emit an `UNAVAILABLE` completion for the intermediate attempt. That is the intended behavior of a logical-call metric.

## 9. Collecting Attempt-Level Metrics

Attempt-level metrics require instrumentation at a layer that knows when each attempt starts and ends. You can use OpenTelemetry gRPC instrumentation/metrics supported in your deployment version, or proxy/service-mesh telemetry. When choosing a source, verify these questions:

1. Does `attempts.started` include transparent retries?
2. Are attempts that fail before reaching the server recorded?
3. Is attempt status from the server, transport, or inferred by the retry layer?
4. Does the retry counter include the initial try? By definition, retry count usually counts only attempts after the first.
5. Are final call duration/status recorded once or per attempt?

If you only have server-side metrics, you can count the attempts the server received. However, that number misses attempts that failed before requests reached the server. Name it `server.calls.completed` or `server.attempts.observed`; do not call it total client attempts.

Do not write a `ClientInterceptor` and increment an attempt counter on every `sendMessage` or `onClose` without verifying the retry layer. A logical call may invoke the interceptor only once even if transport executes multiple attempts.

## 10. Retry Amplification and Retry Effectiveness

Retries increase load on the server. A useful indicator is attempts per logical call:

\[
\text{attempts per call} =
\frac{\text{client attempt count}}{\text{logical call count}}
\]

If the ratio is close to 1, most calls complete on the first attempt. If it climbs to 1.8, each logical call averages nearly two attempts over the measurement window. This signals retry amplification and should be read alongside final success rate and latency.

Additional metrics:

- Retry rate: retries per second by service/method.
- Retry ratio: retries divided by logical calls.
- Retry success ratio: percentage of retried logical calls that end in `OK`.
- Retry exhaustion: logical calls that exhaust `maxAttempts` or deadline before succeeding.
- Attempt error distribution: status of each attempt.
- Final status distribution: final status received by callers.

Do not conclude retries are healthy simply because final success rate is high. Retries can mask a failing instance while multiplying traffic and inflating p99. Monitor attempts/call, final status, and logical-call duration together.

## 11. Prometheus Queries

Suppose custom counters are exported as:

- `grpc_client_calls_completed_total{final_status=...}`
- `grpc_client_attempts_started_total`
- `grpc_client_attempts_completed_total{attempt_status=...}`
- `grpc_client_call_retries_total`

Final status rate:

~~~promql
sum by (service, method, final_status) (
  rate(grpc_client_calls_completed_total[5m])
)
~~~

Attempt rate:

~~~promql
sum by (service, method) (
  rate(grpc_client_attempts_started_total[5m])
)
~~~

Average attempts per logical call:

~~~promql
sum by (service, method) (
  rate(grpc_client_attempts_started_total[5m])
)
/
sum by (service, method) (
  rate(grpc_client_calls_started_total[5m])
)
~~~

Retry rate:

~~~promql
sum by (service, method) (
  rate(grpc_client_call_retries_total[5m])
)
~~~

Attempt `UNAVAILABLE` rate:

~~~promql
sum by (service, method) (
  rate(grpc_client_attempts_completed_total{attempt_status="UNAVAILABLE"}[5m])
)
~~~

Metric/label names are examples only; substitute the actual series emitted by your instrumentation/exporter. Numerator and denominator must share the same source, method, window, and semantics. Do not divide server attempt count by client logical call count and treat it as exact attempts per call if there is traffic from other callers, multiple clients, or attempts that never reach the server.

## 12. Reading Attempt Status Alongside Final Status

| Attempt Distribution | Final Status | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most attempts OK | Most final OK | Healthy service, few retries. |
| Many UNAVAILABLE attempts | Final OK stays high | Retries are masking transient failures; check endpoint health and amplification. |
| Many UNAVAILABLE attempts | Final UNAVAILABLE rises | Retries cannot recover calls; check outages/capacity. |
| UNAVAILABLE attempts then final DEADLINE_EXCEEDED | Callers run out of budget during retries/backoff. | Check deadline budget and failure detection latency. |
| Attempt statuses healthy but final CANCELLED rises | Callers/parents cancel logical calls. | Check caller lifecycle and parent cancellations. |
| Attempts/call rises alongside p99 | Backoff and multiple tries stretch calls. | Review retry policy, throttling, and SLOs. |

A method with 99.9% final `OK` status but an average of 2.5 attempts/call can still be in a degraded state. Callers receive success, but the system is burning far more network, CPU, and capacity than normal.

## 13. Retries and Streaming RPCs

Do not mechanically apply unary retry thinking to streaming:

- A stream may have already sent or processed many messages before failing.
- The client may have already received response headers or messages, making the call committed.
- Replaying the entire request stream can create duplicate messages or duplicate side effects.
- An open stream is not transparently migrated by a load balancer to another instance as a new call.

If stream recovery is needed, design a resume protocol: sequence numbers, cursors, acknowledgements, or resume tokens. The client opens a new RPC from the last confirmed position and the server must handle duplicates via idempotency/deduplication. Metrics must distinguish automatic gRPC retry attempts from application-level reconnect/resume; these are two distinct mechanisms.

## 14. Tracing: Logical Call and Attempt Spans

Traces can represent a logical operation and its child attempts:

~~~text
Application operation
  └── gRPC logical call: Inventory.GetStock
        ├── attempt 1: UNAVAILABLE (instance A)
        └── attempt 2: OK (instance B)
~~~

If instrumentation supports attempt spans, record attempt number, endpoint/service instance, and attempt status in span attributes. The logical call span records final status, total duration, and retry count. Server spans appear only for attempts that actually reach the server.

Do not put attempt number, trace ID, or ephemeral endpoints into high-cardinality metric labels. Those attributes belong in traces. When investigating a retry storm, use exemplars/trace links or sampled logs to jump from aggregated metrics to a specific logical call.

## 15. Testing Retries and Final Status

Integration tests should control server responses by attempt count. Example test service returning `UNAVAILABLE` on the first call and succeeding afterward:

~~~java
final class RetryTestInventoryService
        extends InventoryGrpc.InventoryImplBase {

    final AtomicInteger receivedCalls = new AtomicInteger();

    @Override
    public void getStock(
            StockRequest request,
            StreamObserver<StockReply> responseObserver) {
        int attempt = receivedCalls.incrementAndGet();
        if (attempt == 1) {
            responseObserver.onError(
                    Status.UNAVAILABLE
                            .withDescription("temporary test failure")
                            .asRuntimeException());
            return;
        }

        responseObserver.onNext(StockReply.newBuilder()
                .setAvailableUnits(7)
                .build());
        responseObserver.onCompleted();
    }
}
~~~

With a retry policy allowing `UNAVAILABLE`, the test invokes the method once and verifies the client receives a successful response while the server fixture received two RPC attempts. Configure the service config on the channel and use method names matching the Protobuf service.

A test matrix should cover:

1. **Retry succeeds:** first attempt `UNAVAILABLE`, subsequent attempt `OK`; one logical call, multiple server attempts, final status `OK`.
2. **Retry exhaustion:** every attempt returns `UNAVAILABLE`; attempts do not exceed `maxAttempts` and final status is the final error per retry semantics.
3. **Deadline during backoff:** deadline expires before the next attempt; final status `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`.
4. **Non-retryable status:** `INVALID_ARGUMENT` or `PERMISSION_DENIED`; only one attempt per policy.
5. **Caller cancel:** cancel the logical call during backoff; no new attempts are spawned.
6. **Commitment:** after response headers are delivered to the caller, verify you do not assume the call keeps retrying.
7. **Retry buffer/stream:** check buffering limits and semantics once a request stream has sent messages.
8. **Idempotency:** send the same operation key across multiple attempts; verify business side effects occur only once.
9. **Metrics:** logical call count increments by one, attempt count reflects number of tries, final status records only once.

Do not use `Thread.sleep` to guess when a retry occurs. Use latches, attempt counters, deterministic test servers, and bounded deadlines. Actual backoff depends on config/runtime, so do not assert exact millisecond timings.

## 16. Cardinality and Telemetry Security

Metric labels should only contain `service`, `method`, `final_status`, or `attempt_status` with finite value sets. Avoid:

- Attempt ID or retry sequence number.
- Request ID, trace ID, span ID.
- User/account/order/tenant ID.
- Exception messages, status descriptions, or raw metadata.
- Ephemeral endpoint hostnames/IPs if instance counts churn constantly.

Do not log credentials, authorization metadata, or sensitive payloads in spans/logs when analyzing retries. You can record logical endpoint names and normalized instance identities if cardinality policy allows; prefer keeping per-instance details in traces.

## 17. Common Pitfalls

| Symptom | Cause | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One logical call counted as multiple calls | Adding server attempts into client call count. | Separate logical calls, attempts, and server-observed calls. |
| Client metrics miss UNAVAILABLE attempts | ClientInterceptor only sees final onClose. | Use attempt-level instrumentation or proxy telemetry. |
| Final OK yet system is still overloaded | Many retries mask errors and amplify traffic. | Monitor attempts/call, retry rate, and p99. |
| Every retry gets a fresh full timeout | Re-setting a separate deadline per attempt. | Preserve the overall deadline of the logical call. |
| Retries duplicate side effects | Non-idempotent write operations. | Idempotency keys, deduplication, and status lookups. |
| Streaming replays all messages automatically | Confusing unary retry with stream resume. | Design cursors/sequences/acknowledgements. |
| Attempt count lower than server calls | Transparent retries or other traffic sources not counted identically. | Verify instrumentation boundaries and metric sources. |
| Final status and attempt status merged | Undefined metric semantics. | Use distinct labels/names for final_status and attempt_status. |

## 18. Hands-On Exercises

1. Configure a retry policy for an idempotent unary read, bounding `maxAttempts` and retryable statuses.
2. Create a server test double returning `UNAVAILABLE` on the first attempt and `OK` afterward.
3. Write a `ClientInterceptor` counting started logical calls and final status; verify one call produces only one final status.
4. Use attempt-level instrumentation to count started attempts, attempt status, and retry count; document source/semantics.
5. Write PromQL queries for final status rate, attempt rate, retry rate, and average attempts per logical call.
6. Test retry exhaustion, deadline during backoff, caller cancellation, and non-retryable statuses.
7. Verify idempotency when the server may commit side effects before an attempt fails.
8. Generate a trace showing a logical call with child attempt spans and final status.

After this lesson, you should be able to distinguish a logical RPC from underlying attempts, read intermediate and final statuses, understand how deadlines/commitment bound retries, and design metrics/traces without double-counting. Monitoring attempts per logical call alongside final success rate and latency helps operations teams catch retry amplification before it becomes a load incident in a Spring gRPC system.
